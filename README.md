# Blood-Brain-Barrier (BBB) Predictor

Welcome to the Blood-Brain-Barrier (BBB) Predictor repository. This project is designed to explore and predict the permeability of chemical compounds across the blood-brain barrier using computational approaches and machine learning techniques. 

Predicting whether a molecule can successfully cross the blood-brain barrier is a major bottleneck in neuropharmacology and central nervous system (CNS) drug discovery. This repository serves as an educational and experimental workspace to process molecular data, analyze physicochemical properties, and implement predictive modeling workflows.

## What You Will Find Here

* Data Preprocessing: Scripts and workflows to handle molecular representations, parse structural formats like SMILES, and clean dataset features.
* Predictive Modeling: Implementation of machine learning or deep learning pipelines aimed at classifying chemical compounds into BBB-permeant and non-permeant categories.
* Exploratory Data Analysis: Notebooks and code snippets examining correlations between molecular descriptors and barrier penetration behavior.

## Limitations of the Model

While computational models provide a fast and cost-effective way to screen large chemical libraries, it is crucial to acknowledge their inherent limitations:

1. **Dataset Bias and Imbalance:** Publicly available BBB datasets often suffer from class imbalances, skewed distributions, or historical inconsistencies in experimental assay conditions (e.g., in vitro vs. in vivo measurements), which can impact generalizability on novel chemical classes.
2. **Simplified Molecular Representations:** Relying heavily on 1D representations like SMILES or standard physicochemical descriptors can overlook complex 3D conformational dynamics, stereochemistry, and exact spatial orientations required for active transport mechanisms.
3. **Exclusion of Active Transport and Efflux:** Most binary classifiers focus on passive diffusion characteristics (such as molecular weight, polar surface area, and lipophilicity). They frequently fail to account for active influx/efflux transporter proteins (e.g., P-glycoprotein) that actively pump molecules across or out of the brain endothelium.
4. **Lack of In Vivo Validation:** Predictions generated by this repository are strictly computational estimates intended for research and screening purposes. They do not replace rigorous in vitro or in vivo biological validation.

## Future Direction

Plans for future iterations include incorporating advanced molecular graph neural networks (GNNs), handling data imbalance through rigorous resampling techniques, and integrating multi-descriptor feature sets to enhance predictive reliability.
